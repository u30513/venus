# Venus — Solana Smart Trading Bot

> **Case study.** Venus ran publicly on Telegram as `@venus_smart_tradingbot`
> from August 4 to September 13, 2026 and was taken offline by its owner over
> the legal exposure of running a custodial trading service (see
> [Why it was paused](#why-it-was-paused)). This repository is the public
> write-up: what it did, how it was built, and the engineering practices
> behind it. **The implementation is kept private** — it handles custodial
> wallets and private keys, and publishing that code would be irresponsible
> regardless of the bot's legal status. Companion site:
> [venus-site](https://github.com/u30513/venus-site).

A single-file Telegram bot (Python, aiogram 3) that let a user buy and sell
Solana tokens in one tap and — the reason it exists — hand a trade to
**Smart Trade**: the bot buys, takes profits in stages, protects the position
with a stop-loss that only ever moves up, and messages the user at every step.

## What it does

| Function | Summary |
|---|---|
| 🟢 **Buy** | Paste a token address (or DexScreener/Birdeye link) → token card with market data → one-tap presets or a custom amount. |
| 🔴 **Sell** | Pick a held token → 25/50/75/100% or custom → confirmation → receipt with price paid / sold at / profit-loss. |
| 🧠 **Smart Trade** | Choose a strategy, paste a token: Venus buys and then manages the whole position automatically (below). |
| 📊 **Positions** | Live value and profit/loss per token from an average-cost ledger; tokens that arrived without a Venus buy are flagged ⚠️ (airdrop scams). |
| 💼 **Balance** | SOL + tokens with USD estimates; hide/show tokens; 🧹 clean-up of empty token accounts (rent recovery). |
| 🔥 **Trending** | Jupiter "top trending" (organic-score), 🚀 top gainers (24h, $50k liquidity floor), 🔍 search by name (verified-first). |
| 👛 **Wallets** | Up to 3 custodial wallets per user, private keys encrypted at rest, export/withdraw/delete. |
| 🕘 **History** | Last 10 trades per user (wallet-tagged), Smart Trade post-mortems. |
| 👥 **Referrals** | Fee discounts (5 referrals → 10%, 10 → 20%). |
| ⚙️ **Config** | Per-user priority fee, slippage (capped 10%), time zone. |

Everything was designed for non-crypto users: plain language, one concept per
screen, every transaction leaves a permanent receipt with a Solscan link.

## Architecture

- **One process, one file.** `tradingBot.py` (~12.8k lines) held the config
  loader, Solana RPC client, Jupiter swap client, all screen renderers, the
  aiogram dispatcher and the two Smart Trade monitor loops. No database:
  per-user state lived in small JSON files next to the script, written
  atomically (`tmp + os.replace`) with rotating backups for the wallet store.
- **Custodial wallets.** Each user got keypairs generated by the bot. Private
  keys were Fernet-encrypted with a key read from the environment, generated
  on first run.
- **Swaps via Jupiter** (quote → swap transaction → sign locally → send via
  RPC). Requests paced at 8/s by a lock-spaced limiter; unroutable mints
  cached for 10 minutes; SOL/USD price had a token→SOL→USD fallback.
- **Telegram.** aiogram 3 with `SimpleEventIsolation` (same-user updates
  serialized — without it a slow tap's `state.clear()` could wipe an input
  flow mid-typing). Every state-scoped message handler was registered
  *before* the catch-all handler (dispatch order matters; a test enforced
  it). Receipts never carried inline keyboards: a tapped button edits the
  message it sits on, which would overwrite the receipt.
- **Strategy invariant.** What the Trade Center showed as the selected
  strategy was always the one managing the money: a running trade pinned its
  strategy, changing it was blocked until the trade ended.

## Smart Trade — the engine

Three presets plus Custom (five numbers: SL, TP1, TP2, TP3, trail):

| Strategy | Stop-loss | Target 1 | Target 2 | Target 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🛡️ Conservative | −8% from buy · trails 10% below peak | +12% sell 40% | +25% sell 30% | +40% sell 20% |
| ⚖️ Standard | −12% · trails 18% | +20% sell 25% | +45% sell 25% | +80% sell 20% |
| 🚀 Degen | −25% · trails 30% | +50% sell 20% | +120% sell 20% | +250% sell 20% |

**One stop-loss that only ever moves up.** It started below the buy price,
trailed the highest price seen at the strategy's distance *from the first
check*, and each target hit pushed it to a step above entry (+1% after TP1,
higher after TP2); whichever level was higher won. After TP3 the remainder
rode the trend under the same trail.

Two monitor loops — one for a fresh position, one for a position resumed
after a restart — had to stay behaviorally identical; the test suite diffed
their source for parity. Each tick (30 s): quote the position → **glitch
guard** (a quote >10× / <0.1× the last accepted one was held until a second
quote confirmed it — a single corrupted Jupiter quote once fired a
take-profit at "+86,792%") → resume reconciliation on the first accepted
quote → new-high/trail → stop-loss → TP1/2/3.
State was persisted *before* any notification, so a crash mid-sale could
never replay a sell or lower a stop.

Every TP and the exit produced exactly one permanent screen: what sold, at
what gain vs target, what was received, where protection now stood, what's
next, and the Solscan link. A failing sell notified ("Sell delayed —
retrying") rather than staying silent.

## Money model

- **Fee:** 0.9% per swap, collected inside the Jupiter swap on sells and as a
  native SOL transfer on buys.
- **Receipts** showed "SOL spent / You received" net of the fee.
- **Cost basis** was average-cost per user per mint; every sell — manual,
  TP, stop, force — reduced it.
- Lifetime revenue while live: **≈0.027 SOL over 87 trades**, all of them the
  owner's own testing. Five users ever created a wallet; only the owner
  traded.

## Safety properties

Things that were reviewed adversarially (multi-agent review workflows) and
locked by tests:

- A Telegram failure could never abort a trading action (best-effort
  notifications only).
- A stop-loss/TP sell was never *assumed* without a fresh quote; during an
  RPC outage the bot was blind and said so, and never sold blind.
- Double-taps: an in-flight guard, FSM data consumed before executing.
- Wallet delete was blocked while a Smart Trade was attached to that wallet.
- A burn-dust feature was built, reviewed (19 confirmed findings: NFTs, LP
  tokens and — during a Jupiter outage — the entire portfolio would have
  been classified as dust) and **deleted** rather than shipped.
- Buys recorded quote-estimate amounts (±slippage); a buy reported a
  friendly "Not enough SOL" screen with the funding address instead of an
  RPC error.

## Engineering practice

- 16 standalone test suites, each importing the bot module directly and
  exiting non-zero on failure — including one that compiles the bot with
  `SyntaxWarning` promoted to an error (a stray `(` after an f-string once
  produced a `'str' object is not callable` that compiled fine and would
  have crashed at the first TP3).
- Standard pre-deploy pipeline: strict compile → all 16 suites green →
  adversarial review workflow for any money-touching change → deploy →
  restart.
- Deployed on a hardened systemd unit on a Hetzner VPS, with a daily backup
  cron for the wallet store.

Infrastructure while live: RPC provider ($49/mo), VPS, Jupiter API developer
tier, domain — roughly $75+/month for ~2% utilization.

## Why it was paused

Venus was a **custodial** bot: it generated and held users' private keys and
executed trades on their behalf, for a fee, for anyone on Telegram,
worldwide. In most jurisdictions that profile can fall under
money-transmission, crypto-asset-service or investment-service rules (MiCA in
the EU, among others), with registration, KYC/AML and consumer-protection
obligations that a one-person project cannot meet. The owner chose to pause
it rather than operate in that grey zone — nothing in the codebase addresses
those obligations, and no relaunch is planned without resolving the legal
question first.
